What is Computer Vision?  
WHAT IT MEANS  
Computer Vision is the way AI works with digital pictures. A picture is made of  
tiny coloured squares called pixels. A vision model studies patterns in those  
pixels and uses labelled training examples to guess what an object might be.  
IN THE REAL WORLD  
EXAMPLE 1  
Phones can recognise faces  
in photos.  
EXAMPLE 2  
Doctors can use AI to help  
spot patterns in medical  
scans.  
EXAMPLE 3  
Cars can use cameras and  
AI to notice signs, lanes,  
people and vehicles.  
KEY POINTS  
1  
AI reads pixel patterns rather than seeing exactly like a human.  
2  
One perfect example is not enough; varied and correctly labelled examples help.  
3  
A confident guess can still be wrong, so important results should be checked.

What is Speech Recognition?  
WHAT IT MEANS  
Speech Recognition is AI that turns spoken sound into the most likely written  
words. A microphone changes sound waves into number samples. The AI  
compares their patterns with training examples and uses nearby words as  
context to make a better guess.  
IN THE REAL WORLD  
EXAMPLE 1  
Voice assistants can listen  
to spoken commands.  
EXAMPLE 2  
Phones and computers can  
turn speech into typed  
text.  
EXAMPLE 3  
Automatic captions can  
create text from videos or  
meetings.  
KEY POINTS  
1  
Sound becomes numbers that a machine can process.  
2  
Noise, distance and unfamiliar voices can reduce accuracy.  
3  
Context helps distinguish similar sounds such as “cookie” and “rookie”.  
4  
Fair training needs examples from many different voices and accents.

How does AI “think” or make predictions?  
WHAT IT MEANS  
AI does not think exactly like a human. Different systems find patterns and make  
predictions in different ways. A language model can predict likely words, while  
game AI can test possible moves and score them. A prediction is a best guess  
based on patterns.  
IN THE REAL WORLD  
EXAMPLE 1  
A keyboard can suggest the  
next word you may type.  
EXAMPLE 2  
A video app can  
recommend what you might  
watch next.  
EXAMPLE 3  
A chess program can  
compare possible moves  
before choosing one.  
KEY POINTS  
1  
Confidence is how sure the AI is about its guess.  
2  
Evidence is information that can be checked.  
3  
Correct means the answer matches the real world.  
4  
A confident or common answer is not automatically correct.

What is Generative AI?  
WHAT IT MEANS  
Generative AI learns patterns from many examples and uses those patterns to  
make new content. It can create text, pictures, audio, video or code. The  
instruction you give it is called a prompt, and clearer prompts usually guide it  
towards a more useful result.  
IN THE REAL WORLD  
EXAMPLE 1  
Chatbots can create  
explanations, stories and  
ideas.  
EXAMPLE 2  
Image generators can  
create a picture from a  
written prompt.  
EXAMPLE 3  
AI tools can help create  
music, voices, video or  
computer code.  
KEY POINTS  
1  
Generative AI creates from learned patterns; its output is not automatically a  
fact.  
2  
Clear prompts help steer the result but cannot guarantee it is correct.  
3  
AI-made pictures, voices or videos can look real even when they are made up.  
4  
Check important information and give real creators credit when appropriate.

SUMMARY  
What is the “Fifth Key”?  
WHAT IT MEANS  
The Fifth Key is human judgement. AI can find patterns, make predictions and  
create content, but people choose the examples, ask the questions and decide  
whether an answer is useful, fair, safe and correct enough for the situation.  
IN THE REAL WORLD  
EXAMPLE 1  
A student checks an AI  
answer before using it for  
schoolwork.  
EXAMPLE 2  
A doctor reviews AIsupported findings instead  
of trusting the machine  
alone.  
EXAMPLE 3  
A family checks whether an  
AI-made picture, voice or  
story is real before sharing  
it.  
KEY POINTS  
1  
Machines find patterns; people decide what those patterns are worth.  
2  
Being confident is not the same as being correct.  
3  
Good AI use means staying curious, asking questions and checking important  
answers.  
4  
AI works best as a tool that assists people rather than replacing human  
judgement.

perfect photo isn’t enough — it needs lots of varied, correctly-labelled  
examples to handle the messy real world. Training is when it learns;  
using is when it guesses. People pick the examples and check the  
answers.  
A microphone turns sound waves into number samples. Speech AI  
matches those patterns to guess the most likely words. Noise, distance  
and unfamiliar voices trip it up — nearby words help, but it doesn’t  
understand the way you do. (Just ask SparX about “cookie” versus  
“rookie”.)  
THINK isn’t one big brain — it’s a word-guesser, a move-tester and a  
choice-sorter, each doing a different job. And three words that are not  
the same: confidence is how sure it feels, evidence is what you can check,  
and correct means it matches real life.  
Generative AI makes brand-new things from patterns it learned. A clear  
prompt steers it, but can’t promise it’s right. AI pictures, voices and  
videos can look totally real and still be made up — so always ask who  
made this, why, and how could I check? And give the real artists and  
writers the credit.  
THE GOLDEN THREAD  
Better examples, not a better brain. Give a machine good examples and it does  
better work — every time.  
Feeling sure is not the same as being right. A confident guess is still a guess.  
Whatever AI makes, check it. Ask where it came from, why, and how you’d  
know.

CHAPTER 3 GLOSSARY  
Key Words  
New words from AI Land. Come back here whenever one feels fuzzy.  
Computer Vision  
The way AI looks at digital pictures to find, match, or label  
patterns.  
Confidence Score  
A number that shows how sure a machine feels about its  
guess. A high number is not proof it’s right.  
Generative AI  
AI that learns patterns from lots of examples and then  
makes new text, pictures, sounds, or other things.  
Pixel  
One tiny square or dot in a digital image. Most pictures  
contain thousands or millions of pixels.  
Prediction  
Using patterns to guess a likely answer. A prediction still  
needs checking.  
Prompt  
The instruction you give a generative AI. Prompts steer the  
result but can’t promise it’s right, and the tool may save  
them.  
Sound Wave  
The tiny back-and-forth pushes in the air that we hear as  
sound. A microphone turns them into an electric signal and  
number samples.  
Speech Recognition  
Turning patterns in recorded speech into the most likely  
written words.  
Training / Using  
Training is when a machine learns from examples people  
prepared. Using is when the trained machine handles  
something new.
